explanation_of_foreknowledge_and_managing_unintended_influences What is known. The author designed and conducted the discovery study using SIA/SUS medication files from January 2018 to December 2025 (142,758 new users who started a cholinesterase inhibitor between October 2019 and December 2025). Its results are public in this project. They include the main finding to be replicated (lower 24-month risk of memantine add-on with rivastigmine than with donepezil; risk difference -4.1 percentage points), its variation by age and by rivastigmine formulation, the proportion of patients lost to follow-up by drug, the distribution of baseline covariates, and data quality problems: unavailable monthly files, a health card number shared by many patients, and the rarity of recorded deaths. The hypotheses of this plan were therefore derived from observed data. This is stated openly: the plan is a confirmatory replication of a known finding, not a test of a hypothesis formed before any data were seen.

What is not known. No SIA/SUS file with a competence month after December 2025 has been downloaded, opened or summarized by the author. No eligibility, exposure, outcome or covariate data for patients who start treatment from January 2026 have been observed.

Actions taken to reduce unintended influences.

1. Separate cohort. Validation patients start treatment after the end of the discovery cohort, and anyone with anti-dementia dispensing at any time since January 2018 is excluded. No patient contributes to both analyses.

2. Frozen analysis code. The analysis will use the code deposited in this project, with the same parameters as in the discovery study (washout, loss-to-follow-up rule, placeholder thresholds, pooling of states, propensity and censoring models, truncation, bootstrap replicates and seeds). The data handling rules were fixed on the discovery data before any validation data exist in the author’s hands.

3. Single primary test. One primary hypothesis with a fixed decision rule (the upper limit of the 95% CI of the 12-month risk difference, rivastigmine minus donepezil, below zero). Secondary hypotheses and sensitivity analyses are listed in advance and are interpreted only with their confidence intervals.

4. Shorter outcome window. The primary outcome is assessed at 12 months, so that the analysis can be completed with data published during the study period without choosing the follow-up length after seeing the data.

5. Order of steps. The plan is registered before any file after December 2025 is downloaded. Download dates are kept in the download manifests, which will be deposited with the results, so the order can be verified.

6. Deviations. Any change required by the data, such as new procedure codes, unavailable months or changes in file structure, will be dated in the design log (docs/changes.md) and reported with the results, together with the analysis as planned whenever it remains possible.

7. Reporting. Results will be reported following the TARGET guideline and made public whatever their direction, including a null or reversed result.

study_design_overview Observational, nationwide, new-user, active-comparator cohort study emulating a pragmatic, open-label, three-arm parallel-group target trial. All comparisons are between subjects (unpaired); each patient belongs to one treatment group, defined at time zero. There is no counterbalancing, randomization or blinding: treatment is chosen in routine care.

Data source. Outpatient medication dispensing authorizations (APAC, file family AM) of the Brazilian Outpatient Information System (SIA/SUS), published monthly by DATASUS for all 27 federative units. Each record is one monthly dispensing and contains the drug (SIGTAP procedure code), ICD-10 code, age, sex, race/colour, weight and height, state and municipality of residence, dispensing establishment, exit reason and an obfuscated health card number, which is replaced by a keyed pseudonym at download.

Treatment groups (strategies). Start donepezil (reference), galantamine or rivastigmine, defined by the cholinesterase inhibitor dispensed in the index month. For a secondary hypothesis, rivastigmine is split into oral (capsules or oral solution) and transdermal patch.

Eligibility. Main diagnosis ICD-10 G30.x or F00.x; index month (first dispensing of any anti-dementia drug) between January 2026 and December 2027; no dispensing of any anti-dementia drug (donepezil, galantamine, rivastigmine, memantine) at any time from January 2018 to the month before the index month, which excludes all patients of the discovery cohort; no memantine and only one cholinesterase inhibitor in the index month.

Time zero. The index month, when eligibility, treatment assignment and start of follow-up coincide.

Outcome. Memantine add-on, defined as the first memantine dispensing in any month after the index month. Under the national clinical protocol, memantine is added when the disease reaches the moderate stage.

Follow-up. From the index month to memantine add-on, recorded death (exit reason 41 to 43 or death indicator), loss to follow-up (more than 3 observed months without any dispensing; months without an available file in the state are not counted), 12 months, or the end of available data in the state of the last dispensing. A 24-month analysis is reported for patients who started treatment in 2026.

Baseline covariates (measured in the index month). Age, sex, race/colour, ICD-10 code, body mass index category, state of residence and calendar month of initiation.

Causal contrast. Observational analogue of the intention-to-treat effect: switching and discontinuation after time zero are ignored. Effect measures are 12-month risk differences and risk ratios for galantamine and rivastigmine versus donepezil.

The full protocol, including the target trial specification table, is in the uploaded file protocol_temporal_validation.md and in the replication package of this project.

data_collection_procedures Population and sampling frame. No sampling is performed: the study uses all records available nationally (a census of the population treated in the public system). The population is all patients with Alzheimer’s disease who receive cholinesterase inhibitors or memantine through the Specialized Component of Pharmaceutical Assistance of the Brazilian Unified Health System (SUS), in all 27 federative units. Each monthly dispensing generates an outpatient procedure authorization (APAC) record in the SIA/SUS, published by DATASUS as monthly files per state (file family AM). There is no recruitment and no contact with patients.

Data collection. SIA/SUS AM files from January 2018 to December 2028 will be used. Files up to December 2025 are already held from the discovery study. Files from January 2026 onward will be downloaded only after this registration, with the microdatasus R package, keeping records with ICD-10 G30.x or F00.x. Download manifests (files and number of rows per file) and download dates are saved and will be deposited with the results. Unavailable monthly files are recorded and treated as unobserved months.

Data preparation. Identical to the discovery study and implemented in the frozen code deposited in this project (pipeline_coorte_alz.qmd):

1. The health card number is replaced at download by a keyed HMAC-SHA256 pseudonym, and the original value is discarded.

2. Procedure codes are mapped to drugs with the SIGTAP reference table.

3. Monthly records are aggregated into person-months.

4. Identifiers shared by many patients are detected and handled with the same rules and thresholds as in the discovery study (pipeline parameters max_reg_pessoa_mes = 20 and limiar_placeholder = 0.05).

5. Age 100 or over is coded as 100.

6. BMI is calculated from weight and height when both are in plausible ranges.

Inclusion criteria. Main diagnosis ICD-10 G30.x or F00.x; first dispensing of any anti-dementia drug (index month) between January 2026 and December 2027; a cholinesterase inhibitor dispensed in the index month.

Exclusion criteria. Any dispensing of donepezil, galantamine, rivastigmine or memantine from January 2018 to the month before the index month (this excludes every patient of the discovery cohort); memantine in the index month; more than one cholinesterase inhibitor in the index month; records of identifiers flagged as shared by many patients.

Expected duration. Files from January 2026 to December 2028 are needed for the 12-month follow-up of the last patients included. Given the publication delay of DATASUS, data collection is expected to be complete by mid-2029; the date of the final download will be recorded. Files revised by DATASUS after download will not be downloaded again unless the revision affects the analysis, in which case this will be reported as a deviation.

sample_size_rationale The sample size is fixed by the registration window and by the number of patients treated in the SUS, not by a power calculation. Expected precision: assuming 12-month risks of memantine add-on of about 5% with donepezil and 3% with rivastigmine, the standard error of the risk difference is about 0.22 percentage points without weighting and about 0.27 points allowing a design effect of 1.5 for the weights. A difference of 1 percentage point would therefore be detected with about 90% power at a two-sided alpha of 0.05. Even with half of the expected sample, a difference of 1.5 points would be detected with similar power. The discovery estimate at 24 months was -4.1 points. These are projections and do not change the hypotheses, the decision rule or the analysis.

measured_variables All variables are derived from SIA/SUS medication dispensing records (APAC, file family AM), aggregated into person-months.

Exposure (predictor). Cholinesterase inhibitor dispensed in the index month, identified by SIGTAP procedure code: donepezil (reference), galantamine or rivastigmine. For hypothesis H3, rivastigmine is split into oral (capsules or oral solution) and transdermal patch.

Primary outcome. Memantine add-on: first memantine dispensing in any month after the index month. Time to event is counted in months from the index month; the primary measure is the 12-month cumulative incidence (24 months for patients who started in 2026).

Secondary outcome (sensitivity). Composite of memantine add-on or end of dispensing (loss to follow-up or recorded death), dated at the first month without dispensing.

Censoring variables.

1. Recorded death: exit reason 41 to 43 or death indicator.

2. Loss to follow-up: more than 3 observed months without any dispensing; months without an available file in the state of the last dispensing are not counted.

3. Administrative end: 12 months of follow-up or end of available data in that state.

Baseline covariates (measured in the index month; propensity and censoring models).

1. Age, as a natural spline with 3 degrees of freedom, plus an indicator for missing age; age 100 or over is coded as 100.

2. Sex.

3. Race/colour, with “not informed” kept as a category.

4. ICD-10 code of the main diagnosis.

5. Body mass index category, calculated from recorded weight and height within plausible ranges (25 to 250 kg, 100 to 220 cm), with a missing category.

6. State of residence (states with fewer than 50 new users pooled with a neighbouring state).

7. Calendar month of initiation, as a natural spline with 4 degrees of freedom.

Rare categories are pooled with the same rules as in the discovery study.

Variables used for eligibility and exclusion. ICD-10 code (G30.x or F00.x); dates of all anti-dementia dispensings since January 2018 (to define new users and exclude prior users); drugs dispensed in the index month (to exclude memantine and more than one cholinesterase inhibitor); number of records per identifier per month (to flag identifiers shared by many patients).

Variables used for subsetting.

1. Age group (under 75, 75 to 84, 85 or over) for H2.

2. Rivastigmine formulation for H3.

3. Year of initiation (2026) for the 24-month analysis.

4. Dose of the first dispensing: the lowest dose of each formulation, for the sensitivity analysis restricted to patients starting titration.
indices No composite scales or indices are created. The derived variables are:

1. Body mass index: weight in kilograms divided by the square of height in metres, computed only when weight is between 25 and 250 kg and height between 100 and 220 cm; otherwise missing. Categorized as in the discovery study, with a missing category.

2. Index month: the first competence month with any dispensing of donepezil, galantamine, rivastigmine or memantine for the pseudonymous patient.

3. New-user status: a patient is a new user if there is no anti-dementia dispensing in any month from January 2018 to the month before the index month.

4. Shared-identifier flag: an identifier is flagged when it has more than 20 records in a single person-month, or when it exceeds the placeholder threshold used in the discovery study (5%); records of flagged identifiers are excluded.

5. Follow-up time, in months: from the index month to the first of memantine add-on, recorded death, loss to follow-up, 12 months, or the end of available data in the state of the last dispensing. Loss to follow-up is dated at the last dispensing month when more than 3 consecutive observed months follow without any dispensing; months without an available file in that state are skipped.

6. Stabilized inverse probability of treatment weight: for each patient, the overall proportion of patients who started the same drug, divided by the patient’s predicted probability of starting that drug given the baseline covariates. The predicted probability comes from a multinomial logistic regression of drug on the baseline covariates. Weights are truncated at the 1st and 99th percentiles.

7. Inverse probability of censoring weight: for each patient and month, the running product over the months of follow-up of the probability of remaining uncensored given drug and time only, divided by the probability of remaining uncensored given drug, time and baseline covariates. Both probabilities come from pooled logistic regressions on person-months for censoring by loss to follow-up or recorded death; time since initiation enters as a natural spline with 4 degrees of freedom. The administrative end of data is treated as non-informative.

8. Weighted cumulative incidence for each drug: one minus the weighted Kaplan-Meier survival curve for memantine add-on, using the product of the treatment and censoring weights for each patient-month.

9. Effect measures at 12 months:

. risk difference: cumulative incidence with galantamine or rivastigmine minus cumulative incidence with donepezil;

. risk ratio: cumulative incidence with galantamine or rivastigmine divided by that with donepezil;

. for H2: risk ratio in patients aged 85 or over divided by risk ratio in patients under 75.

Confidence intervals are the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of 200 bootstrap replicates, with all models refitted in each replicate.
statistical_tests All analyses use the frozen code deposited in this project (pipeline_coorte_alz.qmd) and are run in R. The unit of analysis is the patient; time is measured in months.

1. Propensity score model (all hypotheses). Multinomial logistic regression (nnet::multinom) of the drug started (donepezil, galantamine, rivastigmine) on age (natural spline, 3 df) with a missing-age indicator, sex, race/colour, ICD-10 code, BMI category, state of residence and calendar month of initiation (natural spline, 4 df). No interaction terms. Stabilized weights are truncated at the 1st and 99th percentiles.

2. Censoring model. Pooled logistic regression on person-months for censoring by loss to follow-up or recorded death. The denominator model includes drug, time since initiation (natural spline, 4 df) and all baseline covariates; the numerator model includes drug and time only. Censoring weights are multiplied by the treatment weights.

3. Outcome model. Weighted cumulative incidence of memantine add-on in each arm, estimated with a weighted Kaplan-Meier estimator (one minus the weighted survival curve). This is a nonparametric estimator; no outcome regression model is fitted.

4. Hypothesis tests and contrasts.

. H1 (primary): risk difference at 12 months, rivastigmine versus donepezil. Supported if the upper limit of its 95% bootstrap confidence interval is below zero.

. Galantamine versus donepezil and rivastigmine versus galantamine: risk differences and risk ratios at 12 months, reported with confidence intervals and no hypothesis test.

. H2: the whole procedure (propensity model, censoring model and weighted cumulative incidence) is refitted separately in patients under 75 and in patients aged 85 or over. Supported if the 95% bootstrap confidence interval of the ratio of the two rivastigmine versus donepezil risk ratios lies entirely below 1. Patients aged 75 to 84 are analysed and reported but not part of the test.

. H3: the procedure is refitted in two subsets, each containing all donepezil and galantamine users plus either oral or transdermal rivastigmine users. The two rivastigmine versus donepezil risk differences at 12 months are reported with confidence intervals. No formal threshold.

. 24-month analysis: repeated for patients who started treatment in 2026.

5. Confidence intervals. 200 nonparametric bootstrap replicates resampling patients, with all models refitted in each replicate (seed 20261001 plus the replicate number). Percentile intervals (2.5th and 97.5th percentiles).

6. Sensitivity analyses. Composite outcome of memantine add-on or end of dispensing, weighted by treatment weights only. Untruncated weights. A 1% threshold for shared identifiers. Treatment weights only, without censoring weights. Analysis restricted to patients whose first dispensing is at the lowest dose of each formulation. E-values for the risk ratios of the primary contrast.

7. Checks and decision rules.

. Covariate balance: absolute standardized mean differences after weighting must be below 0.1 for every covariate in every pair of arms. If not, the propensity model is extended once by adding interactions of age with sex and with state; the model with better balance is used, and both are reported.

. Positivity: the distribution of weights and the share of each drug by state are reported. States in which any drug has fewer than 5% of new users are reported, and a sensitivity analysis excluding them is run.

. Convergence: if the multinomial model does not converge with the default settings, the maximum number of iterations is increased. Bootstrap replicates that fail to converge are discarded, and their number is reported.

. Minimum size for subgroup analyses (H2 and H3): at least 200 patients and 30 memantine add-on events in each arm of the subset, the same go/no-go thresholds used in the pilot study. Subsets below these thresholds are reported descriptively only, and the corresponding hypothesis is declared not testable.

. There are no positive controls, negative controls or manipulation checks. The discovery estimate serves as the reference for consistency: the 12-month risk difference of the validation cohort is reported next to the discovery estimate, with the difference between them and its confidence interval.
transformations No centering or scaling is applied. Categorical variables enter the models as indicator (dummy) variables with treatment coding (R default), with one reference level each:

1. drug: donepezil as reference;

2. sex;

3. race/colour, with “not informed” (code 99) kept as its own category;

4. ICD-10 code;

5. BMI category: under 18.5, 18.5 to 25, 25 to 30, 30 or more, and missing;

6. state of residence, with states with fewer than 50 new users pooled with a neighbouring state and Amapá pooled with Pará.

In any categorical covariate, levels with fewer than 30 patients are pooled into a single “other” category. Age (coded 100 when 100 years or more) enters as a natural cubic spline with 3 degrees of freedom; calendar month of initiation enters as a natural cubic spline with 4 degrees of freedom; time since initiation in the censoring model enters as a natural cubic spline with 4 degrees of freedom. Body mass index is computed from weight and height and then categorized; values outside plausible ranges are set to missing before categorization.
inference_criteria Inference is based on point estimates and 95% bootstrap percentile confidence intervals (200 replicates). No p-values are used.

1. H1 (primary): supported if the upper limit of the two-sided 95% confidence interval of the 12-month risk difference (rivastigmine minus donepezil) is below zero. This corresponds to a one-sided test at the 2.5% level in the predicted direction.

2. H2: supported if the 95% confidence interval of the ratio of the rivastigmine versus donepezil risk ratios (85 or over versus under 75) lies entirely below 1.

3. H3 and the galantamine contrasts: estimation only, interpreted with their confidence intervals, without a decision threshold.

Multiplicity: H1 is the single primary hypothesis, so no correction is applied to it. H2 is secondary and is interpreted as supportive evidence only, whatever the result of H1. Sensitivity and subgroup analyses are not corrected for multiplicity and are not used to support or reject H1; they are reported in full. Estimates are also reported on the risk ratio scale, with E-values for the primary contrast.
data_exclusion_criteria Included: all patients in the SIA/SUS medication files with a main diagnosis of ICD-10 G30.x or F00.x whose first dispensing of any anti-dementia drug occurs between January 2026 and December 2027 and includes a cholinesterase inhibitor.

Excluded:

1. Any dispensing of donepezil, galantamine, rivastigmine or memantine from January 2018 to the month before the index month.

2. Memantine in the index month.

3. More than one cholinesterase inhibitor in the index month.

4. Records of identifiers flagged as shared by many patients (more than 20 records in a person-month, or above the placeholder threshold of the discovery study).

Outliers:

1. weight and height outside plausible ranges (25 to 250 kg; 100 to 220 cm) are set to missing;

2. ages of 100 years or more are coded as 100;

3. extreme treatment weights are truncated at the 1st and 99th percentiles, with untruncated weights as a sensitivity analysis.

No patient is excluded for extreme values. Patients with no follow-up after the index month are kept; they contribute to the propensity model but not to the risk estimates.
missing_data_handling 1. Sex, ICD-10 code, drug and dates are complete by design (records without them cannot be dispensed).

2. Age: missing values receive a missing-age indicator in the propensity model.

3. Race/colour: “not informed” is analysed as a separate category.

4. BMI: missing or implausible values form a missing category.

5. No imputation is performed.

6. Monthly files that are not available on DATASUS are treated as unobserved months, not as months without dispensing, so they do not count towards loss to follow-up.

7. Missing outcome information due to loss to follow-up or recorded death is handled by inverse probability of censoring weighting, assuming no informative censoring given drug, time and baseline covariates.

8. Deaths not recorded in the dispensing data cannot be identified. The composite outcome sensitivity analysis counts them, together with other losses, as events.
exploratory_analyses Secondary and exploratory analyses, reported in full but not used to support or reject H1:

1. 24-month weighted cumulative incidence and contrasts for patients who started treatment in 2026.

2. Rivastigmine versus galantamine contrast at 12 months.

3. Results in patients aged 75 to 84.

4. Comparison of the 12-month risk difference of the validation cohort with the 12-month estimate of the discovery cohort, with the difference between them and its 95% confidence interval.

5. Unweighted (crude) Kaplan-Meier estimates by drug.

6. Descriptive analyses of the cohort: number of new users by drug, state and calendar month; baseline characteristics; proportion of patients censored by loss to follow-up, recorded death and end of data, by drug; number of recorded deaths by year; availability of monthly files.
additional_comments Relation to the discovery study. This registration covers only the temporal validation of a finding from a completed study by the same author: a nationwide new-user cohort emulating a target trial with SIA/SUS dispensing records from 2018 to 2025 (142,758 patients). The preprint, the full replication package, the pilot study and a dated log of all design decisions are in the OSF project linked to this registration. The validation was planned because the discovery estimate (24-month risk difference of -4.1 percentage points for rivastigmine versus donepezil) is larger than expected from randomized trials, which have not shown differences in efficacy between the three drugs. Several explanations other than a true drug effect are plausible: confounding by disease severity, which is not recorded; differences in prescribing and reassessment practices between services; and differential mortality, which is almost never recorded in these data. A replication in new patients, with the analysis fixed in advance, tests whether the finding is stable; it does not by itself rule out these explanations.

Analyses not covered by this registration. Results will be interpreted as effects on the risk of memantine add-on in routine SUS care, not as direct evidence of slower cognitive decline.

How Exact P-Values works

List any p-values reported with insufficient precision (e.g., p < .05 or p = n.s.) or reported as exactly zero (e.g., p = .000).

This module uses regular expressions to identify p-values. It will flag any values reported as p > ? or p < numbers greater than .001. It will also flag p-values reported as exactly zero (e.g., p = .000, p = 0.00), which are mathematically impossible — p-values are never exactly zero and should instead be reported as p < .001.

A p-value that is not the result of a test is not flagged: a table note (“* p < .05, ** p < .01”), a figure legend (“Asterisks indicate significant differences (p < .05)”, “Values in bold are significant at p < .05”) or a stated threshold (“p < .05 was considered significant”, “thresholded at p < .001”). GROBID often puts such notes into the running text, so each p-value is judged by the words in its own clause (the text up to a “;” or a full stop), and never left out when a test statistic belongs to it (“F(1, 7) = 8.03, # p < .05”). The column not_a_test of the table says why a p-value was left out (“marker”, “threshold” or “legend”). Notes in other forms, such as a marker after the p-value (“p < .05 (*)“), are still flagged.

This module sometimes flags false positives when sentences discuss how p-value thresholds were set. It can also miss an imprecise p-value that is reported against a threshold (“decreased at the p < .05 significance threshold”).

This module only checks p-values reported in the running text of the manuscript. It cannot (yet) process p-values reported only in tables (as opposed to a table’s footnote text, which the module does see and tries to exclude — see above).

This module retrieves information from repositories checked by repo_check about code files (R, Python, SAS, SPSS, Stata, Mplus, MATLAB).

The module will return suggestions to improve the code if there are no comments, if libraries/imports are loaded in lines further than 4 lines apart, if files that are loaded are not in the repository, if absolute file paths, setwd() or install.packages() are found, and if R code cannot be parsed.

The regular expressions can miss information in code files, or falsely detect parts of the code as a fixed file path. Libraries/imports might be loaded in one block, even if there are more than 3 intermittent lines. The module was tried out internally on papers published in Psychological Science, but has not been formally validated. There might be valid reasons why some loaded files can’t be shared, but the module can’t evaluate these reasons, and always gives a warning.

The module also checks whether the repository pins the R/package versions the analysis actually depended on: an renv.lock file (parsed for the R version and every locked package + its version/source), a sessionInfo()/sessioninfo::session_info() text dump (matched by filename — sessionInfo.txt/session_info.txt and similar, or embedded in a README), or a groundhog::groundhog.library()/checkpoint::checkpoint() date-pin call in the code (a bare library(groundhog)/library(checkpoint) does not count — the pinning call itself must be present). When none of these is found, the report flags it as a reproducibility gap: package versions may drift between when the analysis was run and any later reproduction attempt.

If you want to extend the package to perform additional checks on code files, or make the checks work on other types of code files, reach out to the Metacheck development team.

Checking code requires downloading its file contents. For a paper whose repository is on Dryad, this never succeeds unless a Dryad API key is set: Dryad requires one to download file bytes, even from a fully public dataset (listing the repository’s files does not need one). Without a key, every Dryad-hosted code file is listed but unchecked, and a warning naming Dryad and ?dryad_pat/?dryad_auth is included among the download failures recorded in the manifest.

How Data Check works

This module classifies the files in a repository into semantic types (data, codebook, code, software, output, supplemental, readme, asset, other) and, for tabular data files, extracts each column’s type and summary statistics.

The Data Check module consumes the file list produced by repo_check (or any files added via local_path). Each file is classified with rule-based heuristics layered over metacheck’s file_category() and file_types: file names matching README / codebook patterns win first, then an extension crosswalk, then format-locked extension overrides. For every file classified as tabular data that is available locally, the module reads the file head, describes each column with data_col_facets() and computes summary statistics for numeric columns. Every sheet of a spreadsheet that holds a table is read: a file with several such sheets has its columns under source_file = "file.xlsx [sheet]", and the sheet column names the sheet of any spreadsheet with several sheets; a sheet of notes or a codebook is treated as documentation when the file has another table. Following DDI, a column is described by orthogonal facets rather than a single type: representation (numeric/text/datetime/code — how it is stored), measurement_level (Stevens: nominal/ordinal/interval/ratio), concept (what it measures: reaction_time/age/gender/likert/id/…), role (identifier/measure/condition/timestamp), unit, and a quality state (ok/empty/constant). Concepts are detected by name+value rules (the demographic concepts via data_check_demographic()); when llm_use(TRUE) the model fills concepts and measurement levels the rules left blank. Qualtrics survey exports are recognised (data_check_is_qualtrics()): their extra header rows are stripped so columns type correctly, and the reserved response-metadata columns (StartDate, Duration, Finished, …) are tagged.

The module defaults to rules-only when llm_use(FALSE): columns the rules cannot resolve (ambiguous 3–20-unique integers, ambiguous character columns) fall back to continuous / text respectively. When llm_use(TRUE), ambiguous columns and unresolved file types (other) are sent to llm() for optional refinement, and every file is assigned a study group (ex1, pilot2, shared, …) so that multi-study repositories can be recognised (used by psychds_check). Without an LLM the study group is left NA (unknown).

Column extraction requires files to be readable from disk. Files fetched by repo_check from OSF/GitHub/etc. without a local copy (file_location is NA) are classified but not column-extracted; pass local_path to point at a downloaded copy of the repository.

For a paper whose repository is on Dryad, this means column extraction never happens unless a Dryad API key is set: Dryad requires one to download file bytes, even from a fully public dataset (dataset listing and metadata do not need one). Without a key, every Dryad file stays classified but un-downloaded, column_n is 0, and a warning naming Dryad and ?dryad_pat/?dryad_auth is included among the download failures. A one-time session message is also shown (see repo_check) the first time a Dryad dataset is encountered with no key set.

Every downloaded text file the authors wrote (code, settings, survey definitions such as Qualtrics .qsf files, documentation, outputs) is searched for keys: API keys, access tokens and private keys, which let anyone who downloads the file use a paid service at the authors’ cost. Of a tabular data file only the column names and first 3 rows of the table as read are searched: its values come from participants, but a key the authors set for a whole survey (as embedded data in a Qualtrics survey flow) is exported in every response. The search uses the rules of the secret scanner betterleaks (https://github.com/betterleaks/betterleaks, MIT licence) of high and medium confidence. A key found turns the traffic light red; the report and the keys table give its file, line and type, and only its beginning. Other files over 25 MB and binary files are not searched. A key is never tested with its provider. Every module’s result has any whole key replaced by “keyremoved” (see module_run()).

How Reproducibility Check works

This module assesses whether a paper’s R and Python code can be run on its data. It works in two phases. The static phase (always run) collects the package dependencies the code declares, rewrites the data-file paths that do not work in the repository as the authors organised it, works out the order the scripts must run in, and diagnoses why any referenced input file is unavailable — reporting what a reproduction attempt would involve and where it is likely to break. The execution phase (opt-in, execute = TRUE) then actually runs the code: it copies the repository into a throwaway directory, optionally installs the declared dependencies into a throwaway library, runs each script in order in an isolated subprocess with a per-script timeout, and captures the outcome and full output of each run.

By default (execute = FALSE) the module does not run any code — it performs only static analysis: reading dependencies with the same extractors as code_check, finding the files the code references in the repository, and building a run-order dependency graph. It reports what would be installed and run rather than doing it.

When execute = TRUE, the module additionally runs the downloaded code on your machine — a deliberate, opt-in action gated behind that argument (callr is required for R code). Each script runs in an isolated subprocess so a crash cannot take down the R session; note this isolates crashes, not the filesystem or network. Installs (when install_missing = TRUE) and the throwaway run library never touch your main R library. Each script’s outcome is one of ran_ok, errored, timed_out, dependency_unavailable, not_parsed, run_by_source (an R script another script runs with source(), which is run only inside that script, since on its own it lacks the packages and objects the sourcing script sets up first), run_by_import (a Python file other files import, a module rather than a script, unless it has an if __name__ == "__main__": block), jags_model (a JAGS or BUGS model saved with an R extension, which another script reads) or not_r_content (a file that is not R at all, such as a JSON or HTML dump); these last two are not run and are not failures. python2 marks Python 2 code that was not run. With sandbox = "docker" such code runs with Python 2.7 (option metacheck.docker_image_py2; FALSE turns this off), which modifications records; it is never converted, since that can change results. defines_functions is a file that only defines functions, which is used, not run (table$used_by names the scripts that use it), and not_analysis is a file of software rather than of the analysis: a package’s code, tests, documentation or setup.py, a copy of a tool, a web application, a script a program wrote (table$not_analysis_why says which); neither is run or counted as a script. docker_failed means the container never started, which says nothing about the script, so it keeps the light from being green but does not make it red. needs_a_person is a script that stopped because it asks a person to choose a file or answer a question, or that starts an app, and needs_internet one that stopped because the network is off in a Docker run, or, with internet = TRUE, could not reach what it asked for; download_too_large is a script stopped for downloading more than the limits allow (see internet), and out_of_memory one that, or a program it started (such as a compiler), ran out of the memory each script may use. All four are treated the same way. not_downloaded is a script that stopped on a file the repository lists but that was not downloaded, such as one over max_file_size: it could not be tried, so it is not counted as a script that did not reproduce (summary_table$repro_not_downloaded_n counts them apart). not_read is a file metacheck could not read, so it was not run; the light is then not green, as the file may read and run on another computer, and table$error_message says why, naming a package that is installed but cannot be loaded on the computer that ran the check. empty is a file with nothing in it, which is not run or counted as a script. For a script that did not run through, table$error_message is its error, short (at most 300 characters): the error line of its output, which names a package that is not there, with the address it asked for when it needed the internet, or the time limit it reached; table$reason sorts it into a kind. A timeout means “still running at the cutoff”, not a failure, so raise timeout for legitimately long-running (e.g. Bayesian) scripts. dependency_unavailable means the script’s own package could not be installed from live CRAN, the CRAN Archive, or a named GitHub/URL source — an infrastructure limitation, not a defect in the paper’s code, so unlike errored/timed_out it does not force the traffic light red. The full stdout/stderr of every run is kept in the result’s run_results (the report shows it in per-script dropdowns, capped for readability). The traffic light reflects the STATIC readiness signals by default, but an execution error (errored/timed_out) does recolour it to red — a script that crashes on its own analysis is a genuine reproduction failure. A script that fails with could not find function is run once more with the library() calls the project’s other scripts make added at its top (or the one package that exports the function, or magrittr for %>%), and a script that then runs is reported as reproducible only after that repair.

Dependencies are reported name-only, with the source (CRAN, GitHub, or a base package already shipped with R) — static analysis of the code cannot know which version was used, and running against current package versions is itself informative: a break argues the authors should have pinned versions. When the repository does record versions, a run with recorded_versions = TRUE installs the packages the scripts load at those versions, but it does not change the R version, which remains a limitation of the check.

Run order is derived from read-after-write data dependencies (a script writing a file another reads must run first), source() edges, and numeric filename prefixes as a weak tie-breaker. Ambiguous orderings, dependency cycles, and inputs a script reads but that no earlier script produces (and that are not in the repository) are surfaced as warnings, since these are the situations most likely to make an otherwise-correct reproduction fail.

Python code (.py files, and notebooks and Quarto documents whose code is Python, from which the code is extracted first) goes through the same two phases, reported in its own “Python code” section. Each file is checked for syntax errors with Python’s own parser when a Python interpreter (python, or else python3) is available; dependencies are read from the import statements, with versions from a requirements.txt or pyproject.toml when there is one, and installed with pip; each script runs as its own python process (processx is required), so unlike R scripts, a name one script defines is not available to another. When no Python interpreter is available, the Python files are reported as not run and the R results are still returned. Statistics the Python scripts print are compared with the paper together with the R output and any self-reproducible output (JASP/jamovi, SPSS-Viewer, notebook outputs).

How PubPeer Comments works

This module checks references and warns for citations that have comments on pubpeer (excluding Statcheck comments).

The PubPeer module uses the PubPeer API to check for each reference that has a DOI whether there are comments on the post-publication peer review platform. If comments are found, a link to the comments is provided. Comments by ‘Statcheck’ on PubPeer are ignored, see https://retractionwatch.com/2016/09/02/heres-why-more-than-50000-psychology-studies-are-about-to-have-pubpeer-entries/.

The module requires that the reference has a DOI: the DOI in the paper’s reference list, or one looked up with add_bib_match() before the module runs (see ref_table()). References without a DOI are not checked.

How RetractionWatch works

This module checks references and warns for citations in the RetractionWatch Database.

The RetractionWatch Check module compares the reference list against studies in the RetractionWatch database based on the DOI. If a study in the database is found, a reminder is provided that the study was retracted, has an expression of concern, or a correction.

The module requires that the reference has a DOI: the DOI in the paper’s reference list, or one looked up with add_bib_match() before the module runs (see ref_table()). References without a DOI are not checked.

It is possible the authors are already aware that a study was retracted, but the module can’t evaluate this.

The database can be manually updated with the rw_update function. For more information, see https://gitlab.com/crossref/retraction-watch-data.

How Replication Check works

This module checks references and warns for citations of original studies for which replication or reproduction studies exist in the FLoRA database.

The Replication Check module compares the reference list against studies in the FLoRA (FORRT Library of Replication Attempts) database based on the DOI. If a study in the database is found, a reminder is provided that a replication or reproduction of the original study exists, and should be cited. No reminder is given when the paper already cites that replication or reproduction (matched by its DOI).

The module requires that the reference has a DOI: the DOI in the paper’s reference list, or one looked up with add_bib_match() before the module runs (see ref_table()). References without a DOI are not checked.

It is possible the original study was cited for other reasons than the empirical claim tested, or that the replication/reproduction in the FLoRA database is for only one of the studies in the paper, and not the study the authors discuss.

The database can be manually updated with the FLoRA_update() function. For more information, see https://forrt.org/FLoRA/.
